# Bijou du Glacier — Direct-Booking Marketing Plan

**Property:** 4-bedroom luxury apartment, Residence de Glaser, Saas-Fee, Valais
**Sleeps:** 8 (4 bedrooms, 4 king beds)
**Domain:** bijouduglacier.com
**Commercial goal:** maximise net revenue per available night by shifting the mix toward direct bookings, while keeping the OTAs working as discovery and trust channels.

---

## 0. The one-sentence strategy

Let Airbnb and Booking.com pay to find your guests; use the stay itself, the property's own brand, and the repeat cycle to move those guests off-platform for booking two and beyond — and price so that the direct rate is visibly, checkably better without ever undercutting your OTA rates in a way that breaches parity terms.

---

## 1. Guest persona and positioning

### Who you are actually selling to

You have one primary persona and two secondary ones. Don't try to write for all three at once.

**Primary — "The Organiser."** One person in a group of 6–8 who ends up booking everything. Usually 38–55, professional, travelling with either (a) their family plus another family, or (b) three or four couples who ski together every year. They are not price-shopping in the way a solo traveller does; they are **risk-shopping**. Their fear is not "this is expensive," it is "I will be the one who booked the place with four bedrooms where two of them turn out to be box rooms, and I will hear about it for a year."

Everything you publish should reduce that person's risk. That is why the site says *four real bedrooms, four king beds, a table that seats everyone* rather than *stunning alpine retreat*.

**Secondary A — Premium couples.** Shorter stays, higher spend per head, often shoulder-season or summer. They convert on atmosphere and photography, not on floorplan. Captions 3 and 6 are aimed here.

**Secondary B — Multi-generational family.** Grandparents plus adult children plus grandchildren. Car-free Saas-Fee is a genuine, differentiated selling point for them and nowhere else uses it as hard as you should.

### Positioning statement

> A newly renovated four-bedroom apartment in the middle of a car-free village, sized so that eight people can travel together without compromise — five minutes' walk from the lifts, under the Allalin glacier.

### What you are competing against, and how you win

| Competitor type | Their advantage | Your counter |
|---|---|---|
| Hotels in Saas-Fee | Service, breakfast, spa | Space, privacy, a real kitchen, one bill for eight people |
| Older village apartments | Price | Renovation quality — your photos do this in three seconds |
| Big Zermatt / Verbier chalets | Brand recognition | Car-free village, glacier skiing, and a materially lower price point |
| The same apartment on an OTA | Trust, familiarity | Price. This is the entire direct-booking argument. |

### Voice

Specific over superlative. "Four king-size beds and a table that seats eight" beats "luxurious and spacious" every time, because it is checkable. Never use *stunning*, *nestled*, *breathtaking*, or *hidden gem*. The photography carries the emotion; the copy carries the facts.

## 2. The direct-booking funnel

### The shape of it

```
  OTA listing (Airbnb / Booking.com / Expedia)
        │  ← platform pays for the traffic; you pay the commission
        │
        ▼
  Booking #1  ──►  THE STAY  ──►  in-property brand exposure
        │                              │
        │                              ▼
        │                    Guest remembers "Bijou du Glacier"
        │                              │
        ▼                              ▼
  Review + rank ─────────────►  Searches the name directly
                                       │
                                       ▼
                          bijouduglacier.com  ──►  Booking #2, #3, referrals
                                       ▲
  Social (IG / X / Pinterest) ─────────┘
```

The critical insight: **you are not trying to divert a booking in progress.** That is both against OTA terms and largely futile. You are trying to make sure that the *second* booking, and every booking by everyone that guest tells, starts at your domain instead of at a search box on Airbnb.

### What is allowed, and what is not

This matters — getting it wrong risks delisting, which would cost you far more than the commission.

**Do not:**

- Put a URL, phone number, email, or "book direct" language in your OTA listing text, photos, photo captions, or house rules.
- Message a guest through the platform's messaging system suggesting they book elsewhere — before, during, or after the stay.
- Undercut your OTA nightly rate for the same dates and conditions on your own site where the platform enforces rate parity. **Booking.com in particular enforces parity; Airbnb does not in the same way.** Check what your property manager has signed.

**Do, freely:**

- Brand the property inside the apartment. A welcome book, a coaster, a card by the coffee machine, the door plaque — all carrying the name **Bijou du Glacier** and the domain. This is your property; the OTA has no claim on it.
- Give the guest a reason to type the name into a search engine after they leave.
- Add value rather than cutting price on direct: free late checkout, a welcome basket, ski storage, a lower or waived cleaning charge, flexible cancellation. **Value-adds are almost never covered by parity clauses; nightly rate is.** This is the single most useful loophole and you should use it hard.
- Market the direct rate through channels the OTA has no relationship with: your site, your social accounts, email, word of mouth.

### The off-platform conversion mechanics

**In-apartment (highest leverage, lowest cost).** A printed welcome book on the dining table, in the same typographic style as the site. Front page: the property name and the domain. Inside: village map, restaurant recommendations, lift information, wifi, house notes. Guests photograph welcome books. Budget CHF 150–250 for a well-produced one; it will pay back on the first repeat booking.

Add a small framed card by the coffee machine or on the entrance console — name, domain, nothing else. No "book direct" copy in writing that a platform inspector could photograph and object to; just the brand.

**Departure.** Once the guest has checked out and the stay is complete, you are outside most platforms' communication restrictions for non-platform channels. If you have their email through a legitimate route — a guest registration form your property manager collects for Swiss municipal tourist-tax purposes is one, and it is a real legal requirement here — a single, well-written thank-you email a week later is entirely legitimate. One email. Not a sequence.

**The repeat offer.** The highest-converting sentence you have is:

> *Returning guests get first refusal on their dates for the following year, before we open them to anyone else.*

Send it in December for the following winter. It costs nothing, it is true, and it converts your best guests before they ever see a search results page.

### Targets

Assume you start near 100% OTA. Realistic trajectory:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct share of nights | 10–15% | 25–35% | 40–50% |
| Repeat-guest share | <5% | 15% | 25% |

At an OTA commission plus guest-fee load of roughly 15–20% of gross, moving 30% of nights to direct is worth something on the order of **5–6% of total revenue straight to margin**, plus the pricing freedom that comes with not being on someone else's calendar.

## 3. Rate and seasonality strategy

### The Saas-Fee calendar

The 2026–27 winter season in Saas-Fee runs **1 November 2026 to 18 April 2027**, which is unusually long — the altitude and the glacier buy you roughly six months of winter where a mid-altitude resort gets four. Summer glacier skiing on the Allalin adds a second, smaller high season that most Valais properties do not have.

That gives you **four distinct pricing regimes**, not two:

| Tier | Dates (approx.) | Demand character | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peak** | Christmas–New Year, Feb half-terms, Easter week | Sells out regardless of price; books 6–12 months out | Highest rate. 7-night minimum, Saturday–Saturday. Do not discount, ever. |
| **High** | Mid-Jan–early Feb, late Feb–mid-Mar | Strong, price-sensitive at the margin | Core rate. 5–7 night minimum. |
| **Shoulder** | Nov–mid-Dec, Jan after New Year, Apr | Thin, and the weeks you actually lose money on | Aggressive. 3-night minimum, target occupancy over rate. |
| **Summer** | Jul–Aug (glacier ski + hiking) | Moderate, different guest, less price-elastic than you'd think | Distinct rate card. Do not just apply a flat "summer discount". |

### Rules that matter more than the numbers

1. **Never discount Peak.** If Christmas is unsold in September, the problem is distribution or photography, not price. Dropping the rate on a week that sells out every year is pure lost revenue.
2. **Peak weeks are your annual profit.** Christmas–New Year and February half-term together are perhaps 5 weeks of the year and can carry 30–40% of your annual revenue. Everything about how you present the property should be optimised to sell those five weeks first.
3. **Discount the gap, not the week.** An unsold Tuesday-to-Thursday in March should be cheap. The February half-term week should not.
4. **Length-of-stay discounts beat rate cuts.** A 10% discount on a 10-night booking yields more than a 15% cut across the board — and it lifts your average stay length, which cuts your cleaning cost per night.
5. **Set a floor and hold it.** Below a certain nightly rate this apartment is worse than empty once you count cleaning, linen, utilities and wear. Work out that number with your manager and never let a channel manager's algorithm go beneath it.

### Calibrating the actual numbers

Do not take rate figures from a plan; take them from the market. Concretely, this month:

- Pull **your own** 2025–26 actuals from your property manager: ADR and occupancy by month, and the split by channel.
- On Airbnb, search Saas-Fee for 8 guests, 4 bedrooms, for the week of 26 Dec and for the third week of Feb. Record the nightly rate of every comparable listing. That is your Peak ceiling.
- Repeat for a mid-March week. That is your High benchmark.
- Set your Peak rate at or slightly above the median comparable — your renovation quality justifies it, and under-pricing a premium property signals that something is wrong with it.

### The direct-rate mechanic

Given parity constraints, structure direct as follows:

- **Same nightly rate** as Booking.com. Do not undercut where parity applies.
- **Lower total.** The guest saves the platform service fee (typically ~14% on Airbnb's guest side), which happens automatically because you are not charging one.
- **Plus a value-add** that only exists on direct: waived or halved cleaning fee, guaranteed late checkout, a welcome basket, or ski-locker use. Publish it plainly on the site.

The message to the guest is then completely honest and completely compliant: *same apartment, same nightly rate, lower total, plus something extra.*

## 4. Review generation and the reputation flywheel

Reviews are not a nice-to-have here — on Airbnb and Booking.com they are the single largest input to search ranking, which is the single largest input to your OTA volume, which is what feeds the direct funnel. A property with 40 reviews at 4.9 outranks a better property with 6 reviews at 5.0.

### The mechanics

**Ask, but ask once and ask well.** The highest-yield moment is roughly 24 hours after checkout, when the trip is still warm and the journey home is over. Your property manager should be sending this; check that they are, because many don't.

**Give the guest something specific to write about.** Reviews are hard to write. A guest who had a nice time writes "lovely apartment, great location." A guest who was handed a welcome basket with local Valais rye bread and a bottle of Fendant writes three sentences about it. The cost of a memorable, mentionable detail is CHF 30–40 per stay and it materially changes what appears in your review text — which is what the *next* guest reads.

**Fix problems during the stay, not after.** A guest who reports a broken kettle on Tuesday and has a new one by Wednesday leaves five stars and mentions the responsiveness. The same guest who reports it in the review leaves four. Make sure the guest knows, on arrival, exactly who to contact and how fast they'll get a reply. Put it on the first page of the welcome book.

**Respond to every review, positive and negative, within 48 hours.** Short, warm, specific, signed. Prospective guests read the owner's responses more carefully than they read the reviews — it is how they judge whether you are a serious host.

**Handle the bad one properly.** You will get one. Respond factually, without defensiveness, acknowledge what was genuinely wrong, state what changed. Never argue. A well-handled 3-star review converts better than no negative reviews at all, because it proves the 5-stars are real.

### The flywheel, explicitly

```
  Better reviews → higher OTA search rank → more bookings at higher rates
        ▲                                              │
        │                                              ▼
  Memorable stay ◄── invest margin in the stay ◄── more margin per booking
        │
        └──► guest remembers the brand ──► direct booking next year ──► more margin still
```

Every extra franc of margin from a direct booking should be visibly reinvested in the stay for at least the first two years. That is what makes the flywheel turn rather than just extracting from it.

## 5. Four-week social content calendar

**Cadence:** 3 posts/week on Instagram, 3/week on X, 1/week on Pinterest, Facebook mirrors Instagram. That is 7 pieces of work a week and it is sustainable for a single owner. More than this and you will stop after a month, which is worse than never starting.

**Best posting times** for a European/UK audience: weekday evenings 18:00–20:00 CET, and Sunday mornings — Sunday morning is when people plan holidays.

### Notes on channel weighting

- **Instagram is your main channel.** The property is photogenic and the persona lives there. Everything else is secondary.

- **X drives the least booking volume** but is cheap to maintain and useful for the direct-booking argument, which travels better in text than in images.
- **TikTok / Reels:** you already have a vertical walkthrough video. Cutting it into 4–6 short clips is a single afternoon's work and gives you a quarter of vertical content.

## 6. The five highest-leverage actions, in order

Everything above is optional except these.

### 1. Get the site live on bijouduglacier.com this week

Nothing else in this plan functions without it. Every social post, every welcome book, every word-of-mouth referral currently has nowhere to land, and any of that traffic that ends up in a search box will find your Airbnb listing instead — where you pay commission on it.

The site in `website/` is static: it will host free on Cloudflare Pages, Netlify or GitHub Pages, and take about twenty minutes to deploy. **Do this before you do anything else on this list.**

### 2. Fix the direct-booking URL, and make it a real booking path

Right now the direct button points at a placeholder. Confirm with your property manager exactly what the direct-booking URL is for `mattsupplies.saasfee`, put it in, and then *use the property yourself as a guest* — click through and complete a booking as far as the payment screen. If that flow is confusing or ugly, your direct conversion rate will be a fraction of your OTA rate and no amount of marketing will fix it. This is the most commonly broken link in the entire direct-booking model.

### 3. Produce the in-apartment welcome book

The single highest-ROI physical object in short-term rental. It is the only marketing asset that reaches 100% of your guests, at the moment they are most positively disposed toward you, with no platform in between. Branded **Bijou du Glacier**, carrying the domain, in the same typography as the site. CHF 150–250 and one afternoon.

### 4. Book a proper exterior and lifestyle photo shoot before the next season

You have excellent interior photography and no photograph of the building from outside, none of the village in summer, and none with people in it. The gap matters: the hero image is currently doing a job it wasn't shot for. Half a day with a local photographer in December — exterior at dusk with the windows lit, the walk to the lifts, the balcony with the peaks behind — will improve every surface in this plan at once. Budget CHF 600–1,200 in Valais.

### 5. Set up the review request and the returning-guest email, and then leave them alone

Two messages, written once, sent forever:

- **24 hours after checkout:** a warm review request. Confirm with your manager that this is going out and read the text they use — if it is generic, replace it.
- **Each December:** first refusal on next year's dates for everyone who has stayed.

These two emails will, within three years, be responsible for more of your direct revenue than all the social media in this plan combined. They take an hour to write and then run themselves.

## Appendix: what to measure

Track four numbers monthly. Not more — more and you will stop.

| Metric | Where from | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Occupancy % by season tier | Property manager | Peak 95%+, High 75%+, Shoulder 40%+ |
| ADR by season tier | Property manager | Rising year on year in Peak; holding in Shoulder |
| Direct share of nights | Property manager | See §2 targets |
| Repeat-guest share | Your own records | 15% by year 2 |

Vanity metrics to ignore: follower count, impressions, likes. The only social metric worth watching is **clicks to bijouduglacier.com**, and the only business metric worth watching is **net revenue per available night**.
