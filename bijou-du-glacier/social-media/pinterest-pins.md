# Pinterest — eight pins, ready to upload

Pinterest is a search engine wearing a social network's clothes. A pin keeps bringing traffic
for eighteen months or more; an Instagram post is finished in two days. Travel and interiors
are its two strongest categories and you have professional photography in both.

**Nobody in Saas-Fee is doing this properly.** That is the whole opportunity.

The images are already cropped to Pinterest's preferred 2:3 at 1000×1500 in
`images/pinterest/`. They come from your walkthrough video, which is natively vertical — so
these are sharp, not upscaled.

---

## How to upload one

1. Pinterest → **Create** → **Create Pin**
2. Drag in the file
3. Paste the **Title**, then the **Description**
4. **Destination link** — paste the URL. This is the field people forget; a pin without it
   is decoration.
5. Choose the **Board**
6. Publish

Takes about ninety seconds per pin once you've done one.

**Before your first pin:** claim `bijouduglacier.com` in Pinterest Settings. It puts your
logo on every pin that links to you and unlocks analytics. Boards to create are listed in
`profiles-and-bios.md`.

## Why these descriptions look like that

They read as prose, not keyword soup, but every one opens with the words someone would
actually type into Pinterest's search box — *Saas-Fee*, *modern alpine*, *ski in July*,
*open-plan chalet*. Pinterest indexes the title and description heavily, so the keywords earn
their place in a way they wouldn't on Instagram.

They're also 200–350 characters. Pinterest allows 500, but the first ~50 are what shows in
the feed, and short descriptions that front-load the subject outperform long ones.

---

## Cadence

**One pin a week.** That's it. Ten minutes.

Pinterest rewards consistency far more than volume — a steady pin a week beats eight in one
afternoon and nothing for two months. Eight pins is two months of posting; by then you'll
have new photography from the exterior shoot.

Re-pinning your own pin to a second relevant board after a few weeks is normal and fine.

## What to watch

Pinterest analytics reports *outbound clicks* — that's the only number that matters here,
not impressions or saves. Check it monthly. If a pin is getting clicks, make three more like
it; if the board is silent after two months, the descriptions are probably too vague.
