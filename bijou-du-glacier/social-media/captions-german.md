## A note on the German

I've written these as German, not as translated English — the rhythm and the idioms are
native, and terms like *Viertausender*, *autofrei* and *Perle der Alpen* are the words the
market actually uses.

Two things worth a native eye before you post, if you have one available:

1. I've deliberately written **vier Schlafzimmer** rather than using the Swiss room-count
   convention. In Swiss listings a "4-Zimmer-Wohnung" counts the living room too, so your
   apartment would be advertised as 5½ Zimmer. Saying *vier Schlafzimmer* is unambiguous and
   avoids a German reader expecting three bedrooms. Keep it that way.
2. The register is *Sie*-neutral — the posts avoid direct address almost entirely, which is
   normal for brand social in German and sidesteps the du/Sie decision. If you'd rather be
   warmer, switching to *du* is fine, but be consistent everywhere.

## How to run German alongside English

**Don't create separate German accounts.** One account per platform, posting in both
languages, is right for an audience this size — splitting it halves your following twice over
and doubles the work.

Post German roughly one time in three. German-speaking Switzerland, Germany and Austria are
your largest market by volume; the UK and Benelux are the English audience.

If a post does well in one language, run the other version two weeks later rather than the
same week.

**French and Italian:** the site supports both, and the Italian market in particular travels
to Valais. But don't start a third and fourth language until German is running consistently.
Two languages done properly beats four done occasionally.
